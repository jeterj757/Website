Working distinctions

ConstructQuestion it answersDo not confuse it with
Implicit preferenceWhat rapid associations appear on an indirect task?Conscious belief, discriminatory behavior, or a stable personality trait.
PrejudiceWhat negative evaluation is directed toward an outgroup?Policy disagreement alone or every form of intergroup conflict.
Affective polarizationHow strongly do partisans dislike or distrust the opposing party?Ideological distance, support for violence, or antidemocratic action.
RadicalizationWhat process moves someone toward increasingly extreme commitments or methods?Ordinary dissent, anger, activism, or merely holding an unpopular belief.
DeradicalizationHas commitment to an extremist ideology changed?Disengagement: stopping participation or violence without necessarily changing ideology.
DepolarizationHas hostility, social distance, misperception, or division decreased?Agreement, political passivity, or compromising basic rights.

Questions worth developing

Which outcomes should an intervention target: implicit association, explicit prejudice, behavior, affective polarization, support for violence, democratic norms, or disengagement?
When does nonjudgmental narrative exchange create reflection, and when does it place an unfair emotional burden on members of a targeted group?
How do shame, humiliation, threatened status, belonging, grievance, and perceived moral injury interact with identity and group norms?
Can clinicians borrow from motivational interviewing, alliance rupture-and-repair, or perspective-taking without pathologizing political disagreement?
What separates ethical influence from covert manipulation when researchers or practitioners try to change political attitudes?

Cross-course concept bridges

Psychodynamic hypotheses

Aggression, projection, splitting, shame, idealization/devaluation, threatened identity, and relational belonging can generate hypotheses. They cannot by themselves establish why someone radicalized.

Learning and cognition

Conditioning, reinforcement, attentional habits, automatic associations, belief validation, and motivated reasoning help explain persistence and possible change.

Humanistic and existential

Meaning, dignity, responsibility, isolation, authentic encounter, and empathic understanding may clarify what people seek from ideological communities.

Systems and ethics

Family, peer, online, organizational, economic, and political environments constrain choices. Ethical analysis keeps power, rights, foreseeable harm, and stakeholder voice visible.

Guardrail: Use these as competing, testable lenses. Do not diagnose political opponents from a distance, reduce racism to private projection, or assume that making people friendlier automatically reduces violence or strengthens democracy.

1 · Foundations: automatic and implicit cognition

Devine (1989) · Stereotypes and PrejudiceAutomatic stereotype activation versus controlled, value-consistent response.

Greenwald & Banaji (1995) · Implicit Social CognitionPast experience can affect judgment without accurate introspective awareness.

Gawronski & Bodenhausen (2006) · Associative–Propositional Evaluation ModelAssociative reactions and consciously validated propositions can diverge and change differently.

2 · Intervention durability and evidence quality

Lai et al. (2014) · Comparing 17 InterventionsSome procedures shift immediate implicit racial preferences.

Lai et al. (2016) · Effectiveness Across TimeImmediate effects did not persist after delays of hours to days.

Paluck & Green (2009) · What Works?Many common programs lacked persuasive causal evidence; field rigor matters.

Paluck et al. (2021) · Progress and ChallengesUpdated review emphasizing limited follow-up, publication bias, and weak outcome links.

3 · Conversation and deep canvassing

Broockman & Kalla (2016) · Durably Reducing TransphobiaA structured perspective-taking conversation produced a durable attitude effect.

Kalla & Broockman (2020) · Reducing Exclusionary AttitudesNonjudgmental narrative exchange outperformed arguments alone across field experiments.

4 · Large-scale depolarization

Voelkel et al. (2023) · Depolarization and Democratic AttitudesReducing partisan animosity did not necessarily reduce antidemocratic attitudes or support for violence.

Voelkel et al. (2024) · Megastudy of 25 TreatmentsInterventions affected different political outcomes differently; outcome choice is part of the theory.
